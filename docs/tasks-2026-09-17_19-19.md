## Finding: the experiment tested the wrong thing

The run answers “can a (1+λ) hill climber find a good 257-parameter network from scratch?”
(no) rather than “does extra capacity beat the linear plateau?”, which is the question that
justified the task. Two reasons, both about the search rather than the model:

1. **Bounded output at init.** The linear scorer drives the softmax by pushing one weight to
   about 8, which one or two `sigma = 0.2` steps achieve. The MLP output is a sum of eight
   `tanh` units scaled by output weights near ±0.18, so the whole score range starts around
   ±1.4 and the softmax is nearly flat. Getting to a decisive preference needs the output
   layer to grow by an order of magnitude, at 0.05 per generation.
2. **257 dimensions, 16 candidates.** Best-of-16 random directions is a weak search in that
   space. The linear model had 30 dimensions and 24 candidates.

Also: wall clock in the scratch log is misleading. Generations 1–14 took about 20 s each;
15–20 report thousands of seconds because the machine slept overnight.
